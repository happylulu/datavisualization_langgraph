The research hypothesis is that the application of deep learning techniques can significantly improve the prediction and analysis of clinical trial outcomes in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC). The objectives of this study are to evaluate the efficacy of machine learning models in predicting clinical outcomes and to compare these models with traditional statistical methods to determine their potential advantages in NSCLC clinical trials.
Recent advancements in deep learning have shown promising results in various applications, including medical imaging and predictive analytics. In the context of NSCLC, these techniques offer potential improvements in accuracy and efficiency over traditional methods. Key studies have demonstrated the ability of deep learning models to handle complex datasets and provide insights that were previously unattainable. However, challenges remain, including the need for large annotated datasets and the interpretability of model predictions.
The methodology of this research involved collecting data from NSCLC clinical trials and applying preprocessing steps to prepare the dataset for analysis. Missing values were addressed using imputation techniques, categorical variables were encoded using one-hot encoding, and numerical features were normalized to ensure comparability. Model selection focused on Random Forest and Logistic Regression due to their robustness and interpretability, respectively. These models were trained and evaluated on the dataset to assess their performance.
